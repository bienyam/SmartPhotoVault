TidyAway simplify plan

Response to your TestFlight review, 11/20/2026. Built from four Fable 5.2 reviews: an Apple-style UX designer, a senior iOS engineer, a pricing expert, and a click-through tester. Nothing has been changed in the app yet.

The short version. Today the app has 5 tabs, about 8 different names for "duplicates", 3 duplicate screens that can't actually delete, and headlines that open instructions. Getting rid of 50 screenshots takes about 56 taps. Nothing tells the user that deleted items stay in Recently Deleted, so a panicked user frees no space at all.

The fix. Three tabs. One big Free up space button. A list sorted by size that's ready in seconds, "Select all", one sign-off, and one Apple confirmation. Then a single clear last step: empty Recently Deleted. About 4 taps from opening the app to space back.

Tagline pick: "Storage full? Get your camera back."

3. The "Storage Full" rescue, tap by tap

  1. Open the app. The Free up tab says "Storage full? Get your camera back."
  2. Tap Free up space. (First time only: Apple asks for Photos access.) In seconds you see a list with the biggest videos at the top, with thumbnails and sizes. Duplicates fill in underneath as the background check finishes.
  3. Tap items, or Select all in a section. A bar stays at the bottom: "4 selected · 3.2 GB". Favorites can't be selected.
  4. Tick the one-line sign-off in that bottom bar, then tap Delete 4 items. The full wording is behind (i).
  5. Apple's own confirmation appears once for the whole batch. Tap Delete.
  6. The result screen says "3.2 GB is waiting in Recently Deleted. Empty it to free the space now," with 3 picture steps: Albums → Recently Deleted → Delete All. We never say "freed" until the app re-checks free space.

Today: 6 taps plus 2 system dialogs before the first duplicate is gone, about 56 taps for 50 screenshots, and no space freed. After: about 4 taps plus Apple's dialog, then one Recently Deleted step that we explain.

The one thing no app can fix. Apple keeps deleted photos in Recently Deleted for 30 days, and apps cannot empty it or open it directly. Every cleaner app runs into this. We'll be the one that says so plainly and makes the last step easy. Space from iCloud Drive downloads is freed instantly, so that stays as an extra rescue option.

4. Free vs Pro (recommended split)

The principle: getting out of trouble is always free, and Pro is for archiving at scale and power tools. Competitors charge weekly fees just to delete (their #1 complaint). We do the opposite: free cleanup, a one-time Pro.

6b. Archive connections: sign in, don't browse folders

What you saw. Choosing Google Drive just opened a list of folders on the phone, with no sign-in. That's because every destination today goes through Apple's Files folder picker. The app never asks for access; it only shows a line of instructions first (PhotoArchiveView.swift:59, 155-157). Apple's forums also report that third-party drives are often greyed out when an app asks for a folder.

New rule: every outside service connects with its own sign-in and grant-access screen. The user never browses folders. TidyAway creates a "TidyAway Archive" folder by itself.

Cloud services (Google Drive first)

  1. Archive → Where to? → Google Drive.
  2. Google's own sign-in opens and asks: "Allow TidyAway to see and add only the files it creates?" Tap Allow.
  3. Done. The screen shows "Connected: you@gmail.com · TidyAway Archive". Disconnect is one tap.

It uses Google's narrowest Drive permission (drive.file). TidyAway can't see, change or delete anything else in the Drive, so "write only" is enforced by Google, not just promised. Google classes it as non-sensitive, so its review is lighter, but Google must approve our sign-in screen before public launch. Dropbox and OneDrive can follow later the same way.

Why not make people set up Windows file sharing? Recent Windows 11 versions require a real account password for shared folders. The PIN or Microsoft-account sign-in most people use doesn't work, and it's one of the most common complaint threads on Microsoft's own forums. It can't be done without a page of instructions, which is exactly what we're removing. A Receiver app avoids that.

Should we tell people to buy specific storage? ★ No push, just one optional tip. We work with what people already own. A small (i) under "Drive" says: "No drive yet? A portable USB-C SSD formatted as exFAT works best. Spinning drives may need power." No brands and no links, which keeps it helpful rather than salesy. If a drive is NTFS, TidyAway explains it plainly. Since iOS 18 the iPhone can reformat a drive itself (Files → hold the drive → Erase → ExFAT), but that wipes the drive, so we say so first and suggest copying anything on it elsewhere. ExFAT then works on iPhone, Windows and Mac. TidyAway cannot write NTFS itself: iOS gives apps no access to the raw drive, only to files the system already understands.

Not yet verified on a device: drive power limits on iPhone, whether the app can recognise the drive again after replugging (Apple says no reliable fix, so plan to re-pick), and the format checks on external drives. Proof comes from your iPhone plus any USB-C stick.

Still via Files

iCloud Drive and "Other location" keep the Files picker, because iCloud is Apple's own and already signed in. They gain a "test write" check right after choosing.

Write-only message (every destination)

Shown on the Archive screen and in the sign-off: "TidyAway only adds copies to your archive. It never deletes or changes anything there, by design." The code matches the message: no delete call is ever made against an archive destination (the only file it removes is its own unfinished temporary copy).
