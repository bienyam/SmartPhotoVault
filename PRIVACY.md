# TidyAway privacy policy

TidyAway helps you free up space on your iPhone. It works on your iPhone. It has no account system, no analytics, and no advertising, and it never sends your photos, your library information, or your activity to the developer.

## Photos access

TidyAway reads the photos and videos you allow it to through Apple Photos. You can give full or limited access, and change it at any time in Settings.

A scan builds an inventory on your iPhone. It can include Apple Photos identifiers, file names, dates, favorite status, media types, resource sizes and fingerprints (hashes), the names of your albums, and an approximate location rounded to about 1 km. The inventory and an activity log are kept in the app's local storage and are removed when you delete the app. The app does not keep separate copies of your photos.

To suggest groups, TidyAway can look at small versions of your photos with the image recognition built into iOS. This happens on your iPhone. The app keeps short labels (such as "beach" or "receipt"), sharpness and brightness numbers, and which photos look alike. It also keeps whether a face appears in a photo, without identifying anyone, so photos of people are left out of Receipts & documents. It never keeps a picture and does not identify people.

With TidyAway Pro, the app can also look at faces in small copies of look-alike photos to suggest which one to keep (whether eyes are open and how clear faces are; it does not identify anyone), and read the words in your screenshots to sort them into groups such as login codes, receipts and expired tickets. This happens on your iPhone. Face scores are used on screen and not saved. For screenshots, only the group each one belongs to is kept; the words themselves are never stored, logged, or sent.

When you choose a place on the Archive tab, the map comes from Apple Maps. Your photo locations stay on your iPhone.

## Deleting

TidyAway deletes nothing on its own. When you select items, sign off, and approve, the app asks Apple Photos to delete exactly those items, and Apple shows its own confirmation. Apple normally keeps deleted items in Recently Deleted for up to 30 days. If iCloud Photos is on, the deletion also applies to iCloud Photos and your other devices. Favorites are never deleted, and one copy of every duplicate is always kept. The approval, item count, and outcome are recorded in the local activity log, without file names.

If you approve removing a downloaded iCloud Drive file, the app asks iCloud Drive to remove the downloaded copy from your iPhone. The file stays in iCloud Drive.

## Archiving

When you approve an archive, the app copies the items you selected to the place you chose: a drive plugged into your iPhone, a folder in Files (such as iCloud Drive or a file server), or a connected Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox account. If an original is stored only in iCloud Photos, the app downloads it from Apple first. Each copy is checked against its original. The app only adds copies there; it never changes or deletes anything in your archive.

If you connect Google Drive, OneDrive, or Dropbox, that service signs you in on its own page and gives TidyAway access only to the files it creates (Google Drive) or to its own app folder (Dropbox), where TidyAway can't see your other files. OneDrive grants access to your files so that copies can be saved in a normal, visible folder; TidyAway only adds copies to a TidyAway folder in My files and never opens, changes or deletes anything else. To show which account is connected, the app keeps the account's email address or name on your iPhone; it isn't sent anywhere else. Copies go straight to that service; occasionally an item is briefly prepared in temporary storage on your iPhone and removed after upload. The sign-in is kept in your iPhone's Keychain. Disconnecting removes it from your iPhone; your archived copies stay with the service. That service's own privacy policy covers what it stores.

After a verified copy, the app keeps a local list of each copy's file name, location, size, fingerprint, and date, and the identifier of the photo it came from, so you can find a copy and put it back. Deleting originals after archiving is offered only when the app can confirm the copy is safe at its destination. The list stays on your iPhone and is removed when you delete the app.

## Check-ins and reminders

If you turn on check-ins, TidyAway sends notifications from your iPhone on the schedule you choose. To fill them in, it reads how much space is free on your iPhone and the totals from its last check, and keeps a short history of those numbers (free space, totals by group, and how many items are in your library) on your iPhone. iOS may let the app refresh these numbers in the background; that only reads and notifies. Nothing is deleted in the background.

If you turn on reminders before big events, TidyAway asks for access to your calendar and reads events in the next two weeks on your iPhone, to spot trips and events such as weddings and birthdays. An event's name appears only in the notification on your iPhone. Nothing from your calendar is saved, logged, or sent anywhere. Turning the switch off, or removing calendar access in Settings, stops it.

## Storage Score, Storage Checkup and appearance

The Storage Score and the storage bar are worked out on your iPhone from its free and total space and the size of your photos and videos. The Storage Checkup and Storage insights keep a short history of free space (sizes and dates only) on your iPhone, so they can show how it changed. Your choice of Light or Dark is kept on your iPhone. None of this is sent anywhere.

## Purchases

TidyAway Pro is sold through Apple. Apple handles payment; the app only learns whether Pro is active. If you start a free trial, the app can set one reminder on your iPhone two days before the trial ends, if you allow notifications. It's a notification on your iPhone; nothing is sent anywhere.

## Questions

For privacy questions, use the [TidyAway support page](https://github.com/bienyam/SmartPhotoVault/issues). Issues are public, so leave out personal photos and library information.
